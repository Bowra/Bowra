# Conversation page layout

This article describes the parts of the conversation page that show progress, results, and actions, and the writing progress, status, and sharing in the report toolbar.

## Home page

- The home page is a single centered column. From top to bottom: the title and a one-line summary, the composer, a few "Start from a piece of work" suggestion cards, and then the work that needs you, work in progress, and recent outputs. The composer is no longer pinned to the bottom of the page; it sits right under the title.
- When you move through the page with the keyboard, Tab follows the same order you see: first the composer, then the suggestion cards, then the work list.
- If you can only view the current space and can't start work in it, the suggestion cards are unavailable and a line under them explains why.
- Selecting a suggestion card, opening an item in the work list, or arriving at a new piece of work with files from elsewhere all work as before.

## Finding earlier conversations

- The sidebar (and the conversation list on a phone) shows your most recent conversations. When there are more, the last line of the **Older** group reads **Show older**. Select it to add the next 50 conversations below the ones already shown; the list doesn't jump, and the keyboard focus moves to the first newly added conversation. When there is nothing older, the list ends with **No older sessions**.
- Older conversations never load on their own when you scroll to the bottom. If loading fails, the conversations already shown stay, and **Retry** loads the same batch again.
- The search box at the top of the sidebar always searches all your conversations, not just the ones loaded in the list. A conversation is found by its title or by words from your own messages, so you can find it even after its title was renamed. When a result was found in your message rather than its title, it's tagged **In message** and shows a few words around the match.
- Each result group shows how many matches it has. The Sessions group shows the first five; select **Show more sessions (N more)** to add 20 more at a time. Clear the search box to return to your list; older conversations you already loaded stay in it.
- If one kind of result can't be searched right now, a line in its place says so, and the other results are still correct.

## Running progress

For a quick lookup, name the metrics and period, for example “last month’s revenue and foot traffic”. The answer focuses on those figures; ask explicitly for brand/store breakdowns, causes, trends, or a table/chart when you need them. If a figure or its scope cannot be verified, the answer explains that limitation.

Choose a model in the composer before sending. Your personal selection also updates in other open tabs. Choose **Use Team Default** to clear it. A task already sent keeps the model choice it started with.

In a follow-up, state the new question and which files to use. An Agent selected with `@` receives that current request. A PPTX retained from an earlier message is available source material; it does not by itself request a new presentation. To create a presentation, ask for it explicitly.

- After you send a message, the progress box above the answer shows the same information as before: the first line gives the current stage, the step number, and elapsed time; the next two lines show the steps just finished; the last line shows what's being done now. The box now has a lighter, thin border, the text uses the interface font, finished steps have a gray check, and steps with issues are marked in a warning color. Select the first line to see the full process; select it again to collapse it.
- To stop an answer, select **Stop** at the bottom right of the composer (a gray button, so it's easy to tell apart from Send). The button changes to **Stopping…** until the task has actually ended.
- When the answer is done, the progress collapses into one line, "N steps done". Select it to see the process again. If the task couldn't finish, the progress collapses into one line, "Not finished · steps done · time taken" ("3 of 5 steps" when the plan length is known); select it to see the whole process, and find the reason and any recovery action on the failure card below it.

A failed step stays in the process history even if the task later finishes. The issue count shows steps that failed or partly completed; it does not count retries. When the turn finished and those issues did not affect the answer, the collapsed line says in grey "N steps were handled automatically and didn't affect the answer". A highlighted note appears only when unfinished steps may affect the answer; check the data the answer relies on.

If the turn couldn't finish, the failure card explains the recorded cause. When the stage or cause is unknown, it says so. Follow the action shown: **Retry** attempts the same question again, while **Edit and send** restores it to the composer for a new request. An older failure may only restore the question for review. **Copy diagnostic information** helps an administrator investigate access, credit or configuration problems. **New session** stages the question in a new session. **Resume Plan**, when available, continues the same plan after checking its current version and Space; if that check fails, edit the request instead. A credit error identifies whose allowance needs attention; ask your administrator to check it before another attempt.

Expand **Technical details** for available location, interface-version and recorded timing information. Copy diagnostics for your administrator. Without a failure card, open **⋯** at the right of the session title (on a phone, in the top bar) and choose **Copy Diagnostic Information** to include the session and turn numbers. This menu also offers **Rename…**, **Pin** and archiving. You can add instructions while a task runs. After it pauses or ends, its historical failure does not mean another attempt is running. Check completed steps, available results and document status before resending.

For a model-service failure, the card separates its assessment from the recommended step. It may identify a connection problem, denied access, an unknown model or API address, or a rejected request parameter when the service provides enough detail. For an authentication failure, ask your team administrator to check the model connection's API key and access rights; the same connection is not retried automatically. A configured fallback model may be tried. For timeouts or request limits, try again later. Open **Technical details** once to see the service's error message when it can be displayed safely. Sensitive content is removed; if no safe message is available, use **Copy diagnostic information** when asking an administrator to investigate. An older failure may not have a recorded service message.

When a model call fails, the failure card shows the recorded cause, the HTTP status when available, a screened explanation from the model service, and what to do next. These details remain available after refreshing or reopening the conversation. For a rejected Responses request, ask your team administrator to check the API selection and request configuration; change to Chat Completions only if that gateway or model does not support Responses. HTTP 400 alone does not establish that incompatibility. If the invocation outcome is unconfirmed, automatic retries stop; ask an administrator to verify the invocation before sending again. Use **Copy diagnostic information** when the service does not provide a safe explanation.

## Long-running tasks

- **You can leave the page.** The task keeps running; open the conversation again to see the result. If the connection drops, the page reconnects and shows a failure only once the task has really stopped.
- **Progress.** When no new step has appeared for a minute, the running band says which agent is working, at which step, and how long the turn has run, for example “Still running · Data analyst · step 37 · 18 min”.
- **Completion popups.** When a task ends while you are not in its conversation, a popup appears at the bottom right; choose **Open** to return to it. The first popup asks whether to send system notifications while the tab is in the background; this is only available on secure (HTTPS) sites.
- **Partially completed, and Continue.** When a task reaches a run limit set by your Team, the agent returns what it finished and the step list shows which agent stopped at which limit. Select **Continue** below the answer: the same agent picks up the unfinished part with all of its earlier progress instead of starting over. You can also type a follow-up or “continue”. Each continuation gets your Team's current limits again. Saved progress is kept for 30 days. If it has expired, or the agent or its available tools changed in the meantime (for example, a connected service became unavailable), a new agent starts the original task again and the step list says why. If credit ran out, add credit before selecting Continue.

## Agent collaboration details

An answer-only Plan returns findings in chat without creating report chapters. Separately requested charts and files follow their usual delivery options. After Stop is confirmed, unfinished collaboration topics show that they ended with unresolved dependencies; already verified topics stay verified. A partly verified result can still be useful and does not automatically mean the task failed.

For planned tasks, verification covers the step's original objective as well as its acceptance criteria. If a required check is still pending or its completion cannot be established, the step can be partly verified even when its numbers are correct. Inspect the verification badge for the unmet or uncertain requirement and its explanation; these details remain available after refresh. Expand the step to review its requirements before relying on the result. This does not change your Auto, Plan or No plan selection; existing historical verdicts are not recalculated.

In ordinary chats led by Lead, participating built-in and your available custom Agents can ask each other bounded questions automatically. You do not need to install a plugin or enable a setting. When a task includes Agent collaboration, expand its progress to see who asked whom and the recorded replies. A received reply is not a verified dependency: the requester still needs to check whether it answers the question. Limitations and dependencies left unresolved when a task ends remain visible.

Select an evidence reference to open it in Results. Details are checked against your current access; content that is no longer available is hidden. Refresh retries a failed read without sending another question to an Agent. These records appear within the existing task progress, with no separate meeting page.

Auto keeps its existing choice of whether to plan. Steps executing in a Plan can also ask available peers or Lead for help; Plan mode still waits for your approval before executing. Only currently assigned, available participants can answer directly. A completed or not-yet-started step does not restart because another Agent mentions it.

When Lead asks you a clarification question, answer the existing question card. Supported continuations retain the original work and collaboration records. If an interruption cannot safely resume, the task reports the limitation; opening its history does not restart it.

## Actions under an answer

- **Copy** and **Edit and send** appear as two small icons. Hover over an icon to see its name.
- On the most recent completed answer, the icons are always shown. On earlier answers they appear when you move the pointer over that answer or reach the buttons with the keyboard. On phones and tablets they're always shown.
- After you select **Copy**, the icon briefly shows "Copied".

## Citation markers

Web references in AI answers use a page title or source name when available. Links written as a complete web address display the hostname instead, including in earlier conversations. Click to open the original page. Hover over a hostname to see the complete address, or right-click and choose your browser's copy-link action; on a phone, press and hold the link. Code-formatted URLs remain fully visible. If you need complete addresses in an answer, ask for the URLs as code.

Numbered citation markers in answers keep their position, number, and source. Sources you can't access, or that are no longer available, show an outlined marker with a strikethrough.

When asking about code or a message format, paste the complete example and put it in a code block or inline backticks. When the answer quotes your example, its code formatting is preserved. Only an actual question card asks you to submit a clarification.

## Cards that need your answer or approval

Cards that ask you to choose, add information, or approve a plan have a warning-colored border while they're waiting for you. After you answer or approve, the card collapses into a one-line result that stays in the conversation, including after a refresh.

To answer a question card:

1. Pick an option, or type your answer in **Other, type your own…**. Picking only selects; you can change your choice until you send it.
2. Choose **Continue** or press Enter to send. When a card has several questions, you can leave some empty and the model decides from context.
3. To let the model decide instead, choose **Skip and let the model decide**; on a card with several questions this button is **Skip all**.

When a document task needs more information, the card reads "More information needed · Document draft" and its main button is **Continue generating**. After you answer, the original request, template and data are kept and checked again.

When a Datarc query asks you to choose a workspace, select the intended source and continue on that card. Waiting for this choice does not mean the query returned no data. The same task continues with your selected source after access is checked.

If submitting an answer fails, the card keeps your choice and explains what happened. If it says the result is unconfirmed, refresh the conversation to check whether your choice was saved before submitting again. Otherwise follow the offered recovery action. If the question expired or was already handled, **Ask again** restores the original question for editing; it does not send automatically.

If you send a follow-up while a question is still pending, the assistant can use the recent original request, questions and saved answers to understand the topic. For example, “Make it a report instead” can refer to the weather presentation you just requested. This does not resume the earlier task; use its original card to continue that task.

Where bounded clarification is available, the assistant may ask another necessary question before starting the work, up to three rounds. Answer the newest card; previous answers remain part of the same task. An Agent selected with `@` stays selected. Repeating a submission on an older card does not start the task again. This three-round flow applies before execution.

If the current work cannot support a saved follow-up question, no card is created. The failed step says **Question not created**; add the needed details in chat when the assistant explains what is missing. Earlier results remain available, and the assistant can continue working. This is different from declining another question after the supported post-execution question has already been answered. If an existing saved task fails its access or recovery checks, the conversation identifies that failure separately and keeps your answer; follow the offered action.

For supported tasks, the assistant can also ask one question after one or more delegated inspections or calculations finish. This includes independent inspections that ran together and have all finished with confirmed outcomes. Answer on that original card: the task continues with its saved files and results without rerunning the completed work. If those files, Skills, Agents or Canvas content have changed or are no longer accessible, continuation stops and keeps your answer. Do not send the answer as a new task to recover the old one.

For example, you can ask the assistant to read a PPTX, calculate from its contents and then ask about the intended audience, without editing or generating a file. This is a read-only task, not template generation. If the assistant cannot determine the intended use, follow the failure message to clarify whether you want analysis or a layout-preserving edit.

This applies to supported built-in and Team Agents. A failed operation returns its diagnostic to the assistant, which can correct the request, retry or choose another available tool. Earlier results remain available. Answering a saved question continues the original task; it does not automatically repeat earlier operations or confirm an unknown result. Actions still follow their normal permissions and approval steps. If the original task could not be saved, it cannot resume.

Supported continuation also keeps earlier conversation files and results that were already included when the question was asked. Pinning another file afterward does not add it to the paused task. Unpinning affects future requests; deleting a required file or losing access to it can prevent continuation.

Continuing keeps your original request, earlier answers and the original files. If a file is no longer available to you, the task stops instead of silently switching to another file.

If continuation fails before work starts, your saved answer remains attached to the original task and does not use another question round. Submit the same answer again only when the card explicitly offers **Retry**. Recovery can continue only after the previous attempt has safely stopped; it does not start an additional copy of the task.

When a **Memory suggestion** appears in the conversation, choose **Remember** to save it as a personal preference or **Just this time** to follow it for this turn only without saving; suggestions you can reword also offer **Change it**. After it is saved, the result line offers **Undo** and **View in Memory**, which opens **Personal Settings › Memory**. An undone memory reads **Undone**; if undoing fails, the line says so and offers **Retry**.

A newly shown question stays in the conversation while the task finishes pausing. Until then the card says "Saving progress. You can answer in a few seconds." and its options are unavailable; the card unlocks by itself once progress is saved, without a refresh. If the task cannot continue and retry is unavailable, the conversation checks the original answer's status. A confirmed saved answer is shown as saved; use **Copy diagnostic information** to contact your administrator and do not submit that answer again. If the saved status cannot be confirmed, refresh the conversation to check it before submitting again.

If an upgrade leaves a clarification unavailable, keep the conversation and ask your administrator to check compatibility. The question and any saved answer remain intact; Bowra does not automatically send that answer as a new task.

When an Agent has read your attached Word or PowerPoint file and asks a question, answer on that card to continue with the retained reading. The file must still be accessible and unchanged. A question labeled **Type your answer** expects text, not multiple selections. An unconfirmed operation remains unconfirmed; it does not by itself block the assistant from continuing other work.

## Results area

- On a computer, the divider between the conversation and the results shows a handle when you hover over it; drag it to change the widths.
- When the results area is enlarged, a narrow bar stays on the left; select it to return to the conversation. When the results area is closed, the narrow bar on the right reopens it.
- On a phone, use **Chat / Results** at the top of the page to switch between the two. Results include data tables and files as well as reports.
- In a file preview, **Full view** fills the browser content area on computers and phones. Select **Close** or press Esc to return; keyboard focus returns to **Full view**. For HTML, environments with interactive preview offer **Interactive / Reading**. Interactive supports offline page controls such as filtering, tabs, calculations, charts and games. External connections, submissions and dependencies are blocked. Reading shows available static content. Switching views or reloading restarts the page; Full view keeps its current state. If a script fails or a dependency is blocked, switch to Reading or download the unchanged original file.

Dragging files into the conversation, resizing and enlarging from the keyboard, and the dot for new results are described in [Drag and drop, shortcuts, and hints on the conversation page](conversation-workbench-actions.en-US.md).

## Reviewing and finding document files

When a change uses a verified answer you gave to an earlier clarification, its source says **Your clarification answer** and shows that answer. File-based evidence names the file; a clarification answer does not mean you uploaded another file. Keep checking that the proposed change matches your intent before publishing.

When there is no report, **Results** lists the available document drafts and files. Select one to open it; opening the list does not publish anything. Closing a document review stays closed after a refresh in the same browser tab. To review it again, select it from this list or from the **+** (Open…) menu at the right of the Canvas tabs. New drafts can still appear.

A document that is waiting for clarification or could not finish does not automatically open an empty report. On a computer, choose **Reopen Results** on the right; on a small screen, choose **Results**. Open a listed document directly or use the **+** menu in the tab bar. When you close the last automatically opened review, you return to Chat unless you already have a report or explicitly opened Results.

In the **+** menu, under **Document Drafts in This Chat**, select a document to review its preview and changes. The acknowledgment and **Confirm and publish** stay in the review footer. The object tabs (a title switcher on narrow screens) and the tab’s close button × remain above the review; closing the view does not cancel the document.

The review separates original text, updated text and available sources. Open **About this preview** for rendering details and the font warning for any substituted fonts. A page counts as viewed after its full preview has been shown for about a second. Some drafts require every page to be reviewed because the change list cannot verify all formatting and content; follow the remaining-page count. Other drafts require only changed pages. After reviewing the required pages, tick **I’ve reviewed the changes, sources and preview** and use **Confirm and publish**. Check reporting periods, old sample content and related table/chart values yourself; a generated file is not proof that those choices are correct.

After publication, choose **Open PPTX** or **Open PDF** to open that exact file in Results, where the existing download action is available. An unnamed draft uses a short presentation label and stable suffix. A separate request excerpt helps you recognize it; expand **Your request** in the editor to read the full original request. **New Agent** appears in Agent creation editors.

When native chart creation is available, describe the measure, units and intended position for each chart. A template without an existing chart can use a supported new editable chart. Check titles, axis scales, negative values and missing observations in the preview; chart images remain pictures. Availability depends on your environment's presentation capabilities.

To change an unpublished review draft, choose **Revise in chat** when available. This selects that exact draft in the existing input box without sending anything. Describe the change and send it; the new draft starts from the selected version and keeps its earlier edits. Review the new preview before confirming publication. Published files are not changed by this action.

If generation fails, the editor explains the known reason and shows settled usage separately from pending or unavailable accounting. **Check status** reads the same task; **Return to Chat** preserves your unsent input. Neither action restarts generation or sends a new request. A stopped task is not a draft you can publish or revise. You can review the original conversation before deciding whether to send a new request.

Reopening a conversation restores its original Space. Selecting another Space in another browser tab does not redirect the conversation you are working in.

If a document says it cannot reuse earlier clarification answers, it has stopped without publishing files. Start a new request, include the template again and explicitly state the choices you already confirmed. Checking status or reopening the old task cannot repair those answers.

If a template request was not accepted, no document draft was created by that submission. Review the request before sending a new one; a completed explanation is not a downloadable document.

## Settings in the right area

Where available, a Space administrator can select **Open in Canvas** in **Space settings → General** to open the original Space preferences beside Chat. Week start and data language save as soon as you choose them. Use the object tabs (on phones, the title switcher) to return to an open Agent editor without losing its input. Use the close button × on its tab to close the view; if the name or description has unsaved changes, you are asked whether to discard them first.

Where conversation suggestions are available, select the preferences page and ask to change **Week start** or **Data language**. Finish any manual saves first. Review the suggestion in Chat or beside the original controls, then choose **Apply**; viewing it does not save anything. A confirmed apply or undo refreshes saved values without moving you to another object.

If the conversation stops after saving a suggestion, review that existing suggestion before asking again. A saved suggestion still needs your confirmation; an incomplete conversation does not undo it or save the setting.

Other fields keep their original manual controls. If the selected sending mode does not support settings editing, your message stays in the composer. Closing and reopening a page does not attach its old suggestions to the new page instance; send a new request from the open page. **Refresh saved settings** reloads the page, so finish any manual changes first.

## Agent drafts in the right area

Where available, open a draft or choose **New Agent** to resume one of your drafts or start a new one. Edit its name, instructions and tools in the same page. You can describe a change and generate suggestions without leaving this editor. Review the changed fields, choose individual changes or **Select all supported changes**, and explicitly adopt them.

Use the object tabs (on phones, the title switcher) to move between drafts. Each keeps its unfinished input and saved version. Select the first tab, **Results**, to see results while the editors stay open; select an object tab to return to it. Tabs that do not fit move into **More**. A message targets the object selected when you send it. Switching afterward does not change that message's target.

Saving a draft is separate from **Create private Agent**. After creation, **Start using** selects that exact Agent through the existing new-chat flow, where available; it does not send a task for you. Use the management action to open the created Agent.

Agent drafts save automatically, so closing the tab does not ask. Only while a save is still running or has failed are you asked whether to keep editing or discard the unsaved changes. Leaving the work checks every unfinished open editor. If a save result is unknown, use **Check saved draft** and keep the view open until the saved result is confirmed; do not generate a replacement to recover it.

## Reports

On desktop, open the report toolbar’s **More actions** menu and choose **Use narrower margins and wider text** to give the report more room. Choose **Restore standard margins and text width** to return to the default. This reading preference stays in the current browser and does not change the saved report or its exports. The divider between the conversation and Results can also be dragged to adjust their relative widths.

Tables in chart answers, reports and browser exports use the same open reading surface without an outer frame. Choose Table through the existing chart-type button. Chart settings fit narrow screens and scroll when needed.

Regional time-series figures can link the same period or original row across lines, scatter and Table. Narrowing the period keeps the saved series names and order; missing observations remain blank rather than showing zero. Ask about selected observations carries their complete original keys and exact values.

- A chart response can combine several complementary views of the same prepared data, such as a trend above exact values or a contribution chart beside a ranking. Each view keeps the familiar chart-type and More buttons. On narrow screens the views follow reading order; downloaded documents include every view in that order. Ask for the arrangement you need. When a simple calculated column is added, its equation appears below the figure; missing input or a zero denominator stays unavailable rather than becoming zero.
- In report edit mode, each view in a composed figure keeps its existing chart-type button. Open the existing chart settings, choose **View**, then adjust that view’s title, type, legend or labels. Select **Figure layout** to save vertical reading order, two equal columns, or a main view with detail for a two-view figure. The order and data of every view remain intact. Saving keeps the other views and sources together in the same figure. An incompatible type stays unsaved with an explanation; read-only figures do not offer saved edits.

- Move the pointer across a trend chart to compare visible series for the same complete period. Hover, pinned details and Table use readable unabridged numbers: money up to two decimals, other decimals up to four, declared integers without decimals and percentages up to two. Explicit requested precision can retain fixed trailing zeroes. Table states each common unit once in the header; percentages keep the percent sign with each value.
- Axis ticks use a common scale selected from the plotted range and retain meaningful tick differences. Cards and value labels choose a readable magnitude from their own value. For a known monetary unit, 0.0009 hundred-million yuan can read 9 ten-thousand yuan on a Card and 90,000 yuan on hover. Source inspection/download retains the supplied representation. Grouping, decimal marks, currency identity and compact notation follow the current language, including distinct German and French conventions. Pie percentages refer to visible items; hiding an item updates the shares.
- Line endings show series names, with the actual last period when a series ends earlier. Names that cannot fit remain available in the legend and inspection. Monthly ticks may omit the year when the title or subtitle states a single-year scope; value readouts retain the full period.
- If measures would share an axis with different or incomplete units, the figure shows the existing exact table and explains why. Choose a compatible chart type to compare them. A scatter tooltip states both coordinate units; a heatmap color legend states the encoded measure unit.

- The toolbar above a report is shorter, and thin dividers separate editing, export, and more actions. While you're editing, **Done** on the right is the only filled button, and a short status next to it shows "Saved", "Saving", or "Save failed".
- The report status sits on the left side of the toolbar. An ordinary (active) report shows no status label; to mark it as reviewed or archive it, open the toolbar “⋯”. Reviewed, possibly outdated, and archived reports show their label there; open it to remove the mark or restore the report. In a report someone shared with you for viewing only, the label just shows the status and can't be changed.
- While AI is writing the report, there's no longer a separate "In progress" banner at the top. Writing progress (for example "Updating report · Section 3 of 6") appears on the left side of the toolbar. Finished sections look normal instead of being dimmed; the section being written has a colored line on its left, "Writing “Section name”" above it, and two gray placeholder bars below, and it turns into regular content when it's done. Screen readers read the writing progress about every 10 seconds.
- While a report is being written, **Export** isn't available yet, and a note under the toolbar says "You can edit and export once writing finishes". If the connection drops and is being restored, the writing progress changes to "Output paused, reconnecting". If the connection can't be restored, it says "Connection lost. What was written is saved", and **Refresh report** next to it reloads the report's current content. If you pressed **Stop** in the message box, or the writing failed, the toolbar keeps a note such as "Stopped. 3 sections saved" next to the report status (or "What was written is saved" when the number isn't available) until the next writing starts.
- When the report area is narrow (for example after dragging the results panel narrower), **Export** moves into **More** as an **Export** submenu. When you read a report on a phone, there's a single bar above it: on the left, the arrow back to the results list and the report title (a long title is shortened with an ellipsis, and screen readers read the full title); on the right, the report status, **Share**, and **More**. **Export** and **Edit this report** are in **More**. Once you start editing, the results bar comes back and **Done** is on the toolbar.
- A **Share** button on the right of the toolbar opens the same sharing settings as before (who can view this report). **Copy permissioned link** stays in **More**. Reports shared with you for viewing only, and read-only accounts, don't have the **Share** button (the same applies to report cards in the report library).
- The section menu that **⋯** opens at the top right of a block while editing now looks like the other menus: **Delete…** is red and ends with an ellipsis (it still asks you to confirm), and when the cursor isn't in that section's text, **Split at cursor** is shown as unavailable with the reason underneath. On phones, the menu slides up from the bottom of the screen.
- Revision history names each change as **Added**, **Edited**, **Deleted**, or **Reordered**, and the line below says who made it (for example "Edited manually" or "Updated by AI") and which section it was, instead of internal IDs. Times are written in a short form such as "Sep 26, 14:23". With a keyboard, use the up and down arrow keys to move between entries (Home and End jump to the first and last), press Enter on a change to show or hide exactly what changed, and press Tab to reach the buttons in that entry.
- Citation markers in the report look the same as the ones in answers. Sources you can't access, or that are no longer available, show an outlined marker with a strikethrough; opening it still explains why. A source whose preview just can't be opened (for example, a query result that wasn't saved) isn't struck through; opening it shows "Source is currently unavailable".
- Hovering over a citation marker still shows a source card. Source details opened from a numbered marker slide in from the right, and the page behind them can't be used until you close them with Esc, a click outside, or **Close**; focus then returns to the marker. On phones, they slide up from the bottom of the screen. Document excerpts in source details are shown formatted (headings, lists, tables, and code) instead of with symbols such as "##" or "**"; wide code or tables scroll sideways. Images in excerpts aren't loaded automatically; they appear as a link with the image description, which you can open if needed.
- For sources produced by running code or a query, source details show the data as tables (numbers keep the decimals from the source, and empty values show "—"), and any other output keeps its original line breaks; report charts made from that source are shown too. The source card shows the first rows or lines directly, and the reminder that a source link doesn't prove a figure is correct appears only once. When the source is the result of a code run and that code is in the report, the card offers **View run result**: source details then say which run it was and when it ran, show its result, and let you expand the code at the bottom. Reports created earlier don't have this link and show the result only.
- After selecting text, pressing Esc in the font, size, or weight list opened from **Aa** closes just that list and returns to the formatting toolbar. Pressing Esc again closes the toolbar and returns to the text with your selection kept; the toolbar doesn't pop right back up, and appears again once you select something else.
- The source card that appears when you hover over a marker, the formatting toolbar that appears when you select text, and **Edit with AI** and **⋯** at the top right of a block while editing have a lighter style. In the formatting toolbar, the AI actions come first, and the font, size, and weight options are grouped under an **Aa** button that shows them when clicked. After an AI rewrite, **Accept** is the only filled button, with **Undo rewrite** next to it.
- The source card that opens from the source line under a chart is no longer cut off by the edge of the report or hidden under the toolbar. On phones, it slides up from the bottom of the screen.
- You can ask for a trend chart and a trend Table over the same prepared regional time series, with start/end values, changes, sparklines and original-row details. A monthly dataset without an entity column uses a regular Table. State each measure’s unit; percentage levels use `%`, with their storage shape declared separately.
- PDF figure captions and short warnings about previously generated figures stay with the associated visual, including the final chart or Table in a larger figure.
- You can ask the Agent to show a date column in a supported format, for example `20261028` instead of `2026-10-28`. The source values and their order stay unchanged. If the requested format needs unavailable detail or would make different dates look identical, the Agent retains the existing format and explains what needs correction.
- Dates already supplied as eight digits, such as `20261028`, can also be displayed with separators. Hover keeps the available source value and unit; a Table with otherwise indistinguishable unit columns keeps their original units.
- You can also ask for a different number of decimal places on a chart or Table. For a percentage or currency, the Agent uses the known source meaning; changing display precision does not change the original values or units.
- For a complete standalone chart with one measure, you can ask the Agent to switch between a line, bar, horizontal bar and Table. Some complex or incomplete figures cannot be switched through this edit and retain their current view.
- If a chart edit cannot be completed, the Agent can retry it or explain the problem. During the same live turn, the report is protected from a replacement or rewrite used as a fallback; you can separately request a replacement in a new message.
- If an AI-generated arrangement cannot be used, all valid charts appear vertically in order, retaining their measures, original values and sources. An unverifiable source or measure still needs correction; guessed values are not substituted.
- In a linked figure, click or tap a point/bar or a Table row to highlight the same observation in its linked views. Focus the plot and use arrow keys to select, or press Enter/Space on a Table row. **Clear selection** or Esc clears focus. Selection stays local and does not save or run a query; reload or a data/revision change clears it. A selected observation outside another view's displayed range is indicated instead of invented. Report downloads keep the saved full scope.
- When a figure offers **Start** and **End**, choose actual saved dates to view that period in its participating charts and Table. **Show full period** restores all saved observations. Other views keep their own scope. A range change clears point selection; reload or a version/access change restores the saved full period. This browsing action does not save or run a query. Report exports keep the full saved period; individual PNG/CSV downloads use the displayed period and include it in their filenames. The PNG also displays the period.
- When a chart includes a time-frame player, use its selector or previous/next buttons to inspect the available dates. Playback starts paused, stops at the last frame or when the chart leaves the screen, and stays paused when you return. Changing a date does not save a report edit. Chart downloads and Ask about this chart use the displayed date; a whole-report export uses the saved date. The attached file may include other dates, while the question carries the chosen frame.
- Shared numeric axes keep the same range across participating charts, including when you hide a series or browse a shorter period. Exact values remain unchanged. A saved comparison can reject a chart-type change that would make its scales incompatible; temporary unsupported types explain that the shared scale is unavailable.
- Source notes about an event or reporting period show the statement and its supplied dates in the source panel and downloaded report, with the original file still available through the source link. Charts in an answer and in the report use the same current source checks. Line endpoints show series names; hover shows original values. When requesting charts from a CSV, include the unit for each measure, especially when axes use different units.
- A figure may mark an observed point or supplied time period. Short labels appear in the plot; full captions retain the measure, original value and unit. Missing values show “—” and do not become zero. A period uses existing endpoints and does not imply a business event or target. A reading step can locate its annotation. Filtered-out points or annotations whose sources cannot be verified disappear; temporary incompatible chart types also hide their marks.
- Guided reading offers a short sequence of views. Use Previous step and Next step to inspect them in order; each step restores the full saved period. Report exports include the whole reading sequence. Steps disappear if their data or source notes can no longer be verified.
- **Ask about this chart** brings the question and available data file into the chat draft. Review the reference and send when ready. The file may contain more data than the current chart shows. If the file cannot be referenced, the question is kept and a message explains how to try again.
- For a linked chart, select an observation or a supported time range before asking about it. The editable question and file marker show the selection; hover over the marker for its complete scope. Sending still requires your action. If you choose another observation, untouched automatic text updates; your manual edits stay intact. The file may contain more data than the selection. A changed chart or unavailable data requires reopening the chart and selecting again.
- When a chart includes verified definition notes, open its existing source line to read **Sources and definitions**, the affected measures and literal source text. On phones this opens in the same bottom sheet. If the original text changes or becomes unavailable, the note and dependent chart wording disappear with an explanation; saved values remain readable. Report downloads include the admitted notes.
- Source details and revision history slide in from the right and are a little narrower. **Roll back to this version** in revision history still asks you to confirm first. In reports you can only view (read-only accounts or reports shared with you), revision history shows the changes without roll back or restore buttons.
- Charts and metric cards use restrained borders. Native Table has no outer frame in answers, reports or browser exports. The **⋯** and chart-type buttons retain their existing positions and actions.
- On line, stacked-area, combo and dual-axis charts, move the pointer within the plot near a period to compare the visible series in one tooltip, with each value's unit. You don't need to hit an individual point. Series hidden through the legend and missing observations are omitted; actual zeros remain visible. Switch to the table to check available values without hover.
- Charts with many series now tell them apart with patterns as well as colors. From the 7th series on (earlier if a team chart style has colors that are hard to tell apart), bars, stacked segments, areas, and pie slices get diagonal lines, dots, or crosshatching (lines get dashes), and the legend shows the same pattern. If you turn on **Color-vision friendly** in your chart style settings, every series gets its own pattern or line style. Downloaded PDF, Word, PowerPoint, and image files always use the light chart colors and show the same patterns. A few chart colors were adjusted so that neighboring series are easier to tell apart, so older charts may look slightly different when you open or download them again.
- Metric cards now show values of one million or more as a rounded amount in large type (for example ≈64.0M), with the exact value in small type right below it, so long numbers are no longer partly hidden behind the next card. The rounded amount keeps the value's unit (for example ≈¥8.6M), and the exact value is grouped the way your interface language writes numbers. Percentages and ordinary values below one million retain their exact display. Known pre-scaled monetary units can change to a more readable magnitude; the complete base-currency amount stays below whenever the main line is compact. Unknown units are not reinterpreted. If the rounded amount still doesn't fit in your interface language (for example ≈16,5 Mrd. in German), that card's large number is shown slightly smaller. Downloaded files work the same way.
- Stacked bar charts built from two levels of categories (for example province and city) now draw one bar per lower-level item (city), colored by its upper level (province), with only the upper levels in the legend. Before, the lower-level items could end up stacked inside the upper-level bars, adding up values such as rates. When the two categories actually match one to one (for example store and store area), a plain bar chart is drawn instead. This applies to newly created charts; existing charts don't change.
- When each row of a heatmap would have only one colored cell (for example each store belongs to just one recommendation level), the chart is drawn as bars instead: one bar per item, colored by its group, so values can be compared by bar height. This applies to newly created charts; existing charts don't change.
- When the data doesn't form a flow (no stage connects to another, which usually means figures in different units such as visits and revenue were placed side by side), it's shown as a table with the reason instead of a Sankey diagram, because a Sankey diagram would draw figures in different units as comparable widths. Existing charts like this also show as a table when you open or download them.
- Every slice of a pie chart keeps its full color, so slices and legend entries can be matched again; slices other than the highlighted one are no longer faded. The highlighted slice is pulled out slightly instead, and its share is still shown in the middle. This also applies when you open or download existing charts.
- In waterfall charts, the value of a decrease is now shown below its own bar (increases stay above), instead of against the bottom of the previous bar, and labels on the largest step and the total are no longer cut off at the edge of the chart. Waterfall gridlines now follow your chart style settings like other bar charts, so settings such as dashed or lighter gridlines apply to waterfalls too. This also applies when you open or download existing charts.
- Name labels on scatter charts no longer cover other points. When a name can't be placed without hiding a point, it's left out, so crowded areas may show fewer names than before, and names may sit to the left or right of a point; the point named in the chart's caption always keeps its name; you can still point at a dot to see its name. This also applies when you open or download existing charts.
- When a table is wider than the space it's shown in, its right edge fades out to show that more columns can be scrolled into view, so a number cut off by the edge no longer looks complete; the first column stays in place while you scroll sideways. In downloaded PDF, PowerPoint, and image files, a table that's too wide is scaled down so every column fits instead of the right-hand columns being cut off; very wide tables are scaled down to at most 60% of their size, keep the faded edge, and get a note under the table saying how many columns aren't shown.
- In downloaded PDF, PowerPoint, and image files, horizontal bar charts with many categories no longer squeeze every item onto the page: they show as many items as fit legibly (about 30 for a single measure, fewer when bars are grouped), choosing those with the largest values (by absolute value) and keeping their original order, with a note under the chart saying which measure was used and how many of the total are shown. The report page still shows every item, and you can scroll through them.
- Series you hide by clicking a report chart's legend are also left out of the PDF, PowerPoint, or image files you download, so the download matches your screen. This only affects downloads you start: hiding isn't saved to the report, charts other people see or download are unaffected, and hidden series come back when you reload the page.
- Exported PDF and PowerPoint files use your team's colors: the default blue, or your team's own brand colors once an administrator has published them. Text stays readable on every background, even with very light or very dark brand colors. Your personal appearance settings don't change exported files. A report exported before your team changed its brand colors keeps the old colors; export it again to use the new ones.

## Text and layout

- Long web addresses and identifiers in your messages wrap to fit the conversation area. Their original text stays unchanged when copied or brought back for editing.
- Answers, report text, and Help articles share one body text size and line spacing. Reports use a wider reading measure than answers and Help; the desktop report menu also offers a wider option. Charts, tables, and metric cards use the available content width.
- Report titles and section titles use a sans-serif font by default. If you choose **Serif display** as the font under **Personal Settings › Appearance**, only report titles and section titles switch to a serif font; body text stays the same.
- In answer and report text, a little space is added automatically between Chinese characters and English words or numbers (in recent browsers). Copied text is unchanged.
- The product logo appears once, at the top of the left sidebar: the home page no longer shows the logo or watermark, and answers show only the time above them. The version number moved to the last line of the account menu in the lower-left corner; click it to copy.
- Exported PDF, Word, PowerPoint, and image files aren't affected.

Prepared comparisons can show a range with independent current/reference points beside aligned data columns, or several series panels with shared scales. Click a row or point for exact original values and source details; keyboard users can press Enter on a row. Missing bounds do not become zeros. Scenario labels and result summaries describe supplied assumptions, not a forecast calculated by the chart. These views keep the familiar controls but prevent type changes or Top-N operations that would discard their meaning. Long interval tables and facets continue in reading order in document exports; an individual image states its displayed row scope.

An available Agent may still need an optional Team service for a particular task. If image generation is not configured, you can continue editing or exporting an existing design. For a new poster needing a background, ask a Team administrator to configure image generation or supply a background image. The answer should explain the missing service; it does not mean the Agent is disabled.
